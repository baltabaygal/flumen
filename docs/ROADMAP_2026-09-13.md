# flumen — road to a paper-deliverable release

2026-09-13. Written against flumen v0.1 (frozen 2026-09-08, commit 64f0695) and
the `single_body_new` / `single_body_naive` work of 2026-09-10..12 in
`gw-wl-emulator`. Status of every item: NOT STARTED unless marked.

Goal: a third party installs `flumen`, calls one function with a cosmology and a
source redshift, and gets a magnification PDF that is fast, correct, and
citable against the paper.

---

## Gate 0 — three decisions that block everything else

These are not engineering tasks. Nothing downstream can be sequenced until they
are answered, and answering them wrong means redoing the retrain.

**D1. Which physics does the paper describe?**
flumen's shipped model and ALL its bundled data are `physics_config_hash
c10eca783b41` (pre-cons16). The 2026-09-10 datasets are `61916685cd6b`
(`conc_model=16`, the supervisor's 2026-09-09 adoption). If the paper's
simulator section describes cons16 — and it should, since that is the engine
default — **the packaged model does not implement the paper and must be
retrained.** There is no partial answer here: the concentration relation feeds
every halo.

**D2. Which `kappa_anchor` convention is the product?**
`kappa_anchor=1` (robust/trimmed) is the production default and what flumen's
data uses. `single_body_naive` regenerated everything at `kappa_anchor=0`
because the robust anchor was measured to shift kappa_bar 0.6875 -> 0.7647 at
the hi-struct corner — a systematic displacement of the whole PDF in ln(mu),
not noise. But the robust anchor exists for a real reason (one monster ray
dragging a whole batch), and **the trade between the two failure modes has
never been scored end to end.** This decides which dataset the release trains
on, and the paper must state the convention explicitly either way.

**D3. Which model ships?**
Two candidates, and they are NOT yet comparable:

| | flumen v0.1 | single_body_naive |
|---|---|---|
| bodies | 2 SOSPF x 70,412 params + Hermite bridge | 1 SOSPF, 20,874 params |
| low-mu bound | FITTED edge minus k*sigma margin | PHYSICAL, 1/(1+kappa_bar)^2, enforced by the coordinate |
| tail | POT, adaptive switch | analytic mu^-2 splice, index = 2.0000 exactly |
| KL_y | **0.0022** (973 untouched TEST configs) | 0.0030 (validation, measured m/s/kbar) |
| deployable | YES | **NO** — m, s, kappa_bar are measured per config, not predicted |

⚠ Those two KL numbers are on different data, different coordinate, different
support and different physics. Per this project's own rule, they cannot be
compared as they stand. **Deciding D3 on that table would be a mistake** — see
Phase 3.1, which is the work that makes the comparison legitimate.

Recommendation: answer D1 and D2 first, run 3.1 (a same-support head-to-head),
then decide D3 on evidence. If D1 = cons16, both candidates need a retrain
anyway, so the retrain is the natural place to settle D3.

## Phase 1 — physics consistency (blocks a paper claim, not just the code)

- [ ] **1.1** Write down, in one table, the exact physics the paper's simulator
      section claims vs. what `61916685cd6b` and `c10eca783b41` actually are.
      Include cons14/cons16, the sigma8 top-hat anchor, subhalo model 5, the
      bias window and R_s, and the kappa anchor. This table goes in the paper
      AND in `docs/MODEL_CARD.md`; it is the artifact that makes the model
      auditable.
- [ ] **1.2** Decide D1/D2 with the supervisor. Record the decision and its
      date in `PROVENANCE.md`.
- [ ] **1.3** If D1 = cons16: mark flumen v0.1's bundled data and every
      certificate under `evaluation/results/` and `qualification/` as
      superseded. Do NOT delete them — they are the provenance of the old
      model, and the paper may still reference the comparison.

## Phase 2 — make the chosen model deployable

If D3 = flumen v0.1, this phase is just "retrain on the new data" and 2.1-2.3
do not apply. If D3 = single_body_naive, all of it applies. **2.1 is the real
blocker**: the naive model is not a model yet, it is a model plus three numbers
it reads off the answer.

- [ ] **2.1** Predictors for the three per-config controls, from (z, theta) alone:
  - [ ] `kappa_bar(z, theta)` — NEW. Nothing in flumen predicts this today.
        Reported to look like a tight function of z and sigma8, but that is an
        eyeball from one panel, not a fit.
        ⚠⚠ **This one is dangerous in a way m and s are not.** The bound
        `y_b = (-2ln(1+kappa_bar) - m)/s` is a HARD zero of the density. If the
        predictor over-estimates kappa_bar, the bound lands INSIDE the data and
        the model returns `-inf` where rays exist — an infinitely bad
        likelihood, not a small error. **Fit it, then budget the residual, then
        subtract a conservative multiple of that residual, and add a runtime
        guard that refuses a bound above the config's own predicted edge.**
  - [ ] `m(z, theta)`, `s(z, theta)` — flumen already has these
        (`model/scale.py`, ridge on `scale_features`), but they must be REFIT
        on whichever dataset D1/D2 selects. `s` carries ~3.2% error; that error
        propagates into y and therefore into everything.
  - [ ] Decide whether to adopt `single_body_new`'s GEV/L-moment definition of
        m and s over the median/quantile-spread pair. It is implemented and
        documented (`scripts/gev_lmoments.py`, `docs/loc_scale_gev_lit_review.md`)
        and it is the more defensible choice for a skewed, hard-edged
        distribution — but it has never been trained against, only plotted.
        Cheap to settle: refit both, train one body each, compare.
- [ ] **2.2** Retrain properly: full epochs (the scan stopped at 8 with val NLL
      still falling ~1e-4/epoch), >= 3 seeds so the release checkpoint is not a
      lucky draw, on the D1/D2 dataset. Use RQS, not SOSPF — the coordinate
      scan showed they tie on NLL_y at **1/3 the wall time**.
- [ ] **2.3** Fit the tail handover instead of picking it. `mu_c = 20` was
      chosen by hand. Scan it against a y-binned objective (NOT absolute-log-mu
      binned KL — that objective is why the earlier `fit_blend` refusal was
      uninformative), keeping the `max(width-relative, absolute mu)` rule, which
      is load-bearing: a purely width-relative handover injects a power-law tail
      at mu ~ 1.03 in low-z configs that have no strong lensing at all.
- [ ] **2.4** Re-derive the unit-flux calibration (`<1/mu> = 1`) for the new
      composite and keep the `FLUX_DELTA_MAX = 0.05` trust region. The
      2026-09-07 patch record shows what happens without it: a ~5x median KL
      regression at z >= 8.

## Phase 3 — the acceptance certificate (what makes it "reliable")

Nothing ships on a validation number. The release needs a certificate on the
**untouched test split**, reproducible by a reader from the repository.

- [ ] **3.1** ⚠ **Same-support head-to-head first.** Score flumen v0.1 and the
      naive candidate on the SAME configs, SAME grid, SAME physics, SAME
      metrics. Until this exists, D3 has no evidence behind it and "0.0030 vs
      0.0022" is a comparison of two different questions.
- [ ] **3.2** Per-metric seed bands before any of the numbers below are quoted.
      Measured already on the naive baseline: NLL_u 4.2e-4, but **KL_y's own
      band is 3.1e-4 on a value of ~0.0031 — about 10%.** TLSE's band has never
      been measured, and df=3 was chosen on TLSE.
- [ ] **3.3** Full metric sweep on the test split, per z band: KL_y, FOOT
      (q=1e-3 and 1e-4, paired), PIT/KS, TVS_y and slope flips on the FULL
      support (not just the body — that is exactly the mistake the overnight
      report had to correct), TLSE, local tail index a(mu) at mu = 10..1e8,
      and unit flux. flumen already has the harness shape for most of these.
- [ ] **3.4** Corner behaviour: box lo-struct and box hi-struct vertices at all
      8 panel z's, against their own held-out shells. These are the known-hard
      regions and a reviewer will look at them.
- [ ] **3.5** Extrapolation policy, tested: what the model does OUTSIDE
      `TRAINING_RANGE`. Today it warns and evaluates. Decide whether that is
      the contract, and make sure it cannot silently return nonsense.
- [ ] **3.6** Freeze the certificate as JSON under `evaluation/results/` plus a
      one-command reproduction, as v0.1 already does.

## Phase 4 — usability for people who are not us

This is the half that decides whether anyone actually uses it. v0.1's structure
is good; these are the gaps.

- [ ] **4.1** Keep `flumen.generate_pdf(z_s=..., h=..., Om=..., sigma8=...)`
      exactly as it is. It is the right API and it is already documented. Any
      new model goes BEHIND it.
- [ ] **4.2** Vectorize over contexts. Today the call is one cosmology at a
      time; anyone running an MCMC will want `(N_ctx, N_mu)` in one call. This
      is the single highest-value API change for downstream users.
- [ ] **4.3** Add the three things a user of a PDF asks for next: `cdf`,
      `ppf`/quantiles, and `rvs` sampling. All are cheap given the flow.
- [ ] **4.4** Loosen the dependency pins. `numpy==2.4.3, torch==2.6.0,
      zuko==1.6.0, h5py==3.16.0` are exact; on most machines that is an
      uninstallable package. Move to compatible ranges, and keep the exact set
      in a separate `requirements-lock.txt` for reproducing the paper.
- [ ] **4.5** Benchmark and state the real speed, on CPU, for a single call and
      for a batch. The README claims "milliseconds"; measure it and put the
      machine next to the number.
- [ ] **4.6** Smoke-test install in a CLEAN environment from a git clone. The
      package currently imports from the repo root with `package-dir
      {flumen = "."}`; verify that a fresh `pip install` of a tag actually
      works somewhere that is not this laptop.
- [ ] **4.7** CI: run the test suite + a tiny end-to-end `generate_pdf` call on
      push. Without it the package silently rots.

## Phase 5 — distribution and the paper

- [ ] **5.1** **LICENSE — there is none.** Without it the code is legally
      unusable by anyone else, which defeats the entire purpose. Pick one
      (MIT/BSD-3 are the norm for astro tooling) and check it against any
      constraints from the Vaskonen simulator lineage.
- [ ] **5.2** The data problem: the repo already packs **507 MB** and the new
      datasets are ~1.2 GB more. That does not belong in git. Move datasets to
      Zenodo, keep `MANIFEST.sha256` + a download helper in the repo, and keep
      only the checkpoints and calibration JSONs in-tree (they are ~700 KB).
- [ ] **5.3** Zenodo DOI for the release, cited from the paper.
- [ ] **5.4** Fill in the citation block — it is `TODO: author list` and
      `arXiv:XXXX.XXXXX` today — and add the Vaskonen (2026) simulator citation
      alongside it.
- [ ] **5.5** Update `docs/MODEL_CARD.md` and `PROVENANCE.md` to the shipped
      model: architecture, the D1/D2 decisions, the certificate numbers, and an
      honest known-limitations section (see 5.6).
- [ ] **5.6** Known limitations, stated rather than discovered by a user:
      the tail is an analytic asymptote spliced on (C0 at the handover, by
      construction); the low-mu bound is exact only where the simulator's own
      `bias_weak` arm does not push kappa_raw below zero — measured to break
      only at the hi-struct corner, at 1e-6..3e-4 of rays; and everything
      outside `TRAINING_RANGE` is extrapolation.
- [ ] **5.7** Make the repo public at the tag the paper cites, and make sure
      the figures in the paper can be regenerated from it (v0.1 already has
      `evaluation/plot_paper_figures.py` — keep that property).

## Out of scope, deliberately

Per `gw-wl-emulator/CLAUDE.md` section 0a: no posterior/forecast work, no
h/Om/sigma8 recovery, no mock catalogues, no Hubble-diagram stage. The
deliverable is the PDF and its API. If a reviewer asks for a parameter-recovery
demonstration, that is a scope change to raise with the supervisor, not
something to slip in.

## Suggested order

1. Gate 0 (D1, D2) — a conversation, not a compute job.
2. 3.1 + 3.2 — makes D3 decidable. Cheap: no training, only scoring.
3. Phase 2 — the retrain, once D1/D2/D3 are all answered.
4. Phase 3 — the certificate.
5. Phases 4 and 5 in parallel with Phase 3; 4.4, 5.1 and 5.2 are independent of
   which model wins and can start today.
